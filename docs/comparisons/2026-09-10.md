# Mock ↔ real BoondManager — the salary package

- real: `https://ui.boondmanager.com/api` — production tenant, 9.1.78.1
- mock: `ghcr.io/littlebigcode/boondmanager-mock:0.8.0`, dataset `realiste`, seed 42
- measured 2026-09-10, read-only, ~90 GET calls, from the `insights360` extractor's own client
- sample: 15 resources including one witness carrying 8 contracts, **18 contract profiles**

## Why this probe happened

A consumer asked a plain question: *where is the variable pay?* The repository's
own answer, written in a dbt model, was « Avantages versés — 54 lines for
resource 583 — they live outside the contract and are not extracted ». Nobody
had ever looked for the endpoint.

Two probes later, this report. **The mock was wrong in four places, and three of
them were the believable kind.**

## 1. `/resources/{id}/advantages` — the route that was missing

**200 OK.** `meta.totals.rows` = 54 on the witness resource — the very 54 lines
the consumer's comment mentioned. History runs 2020 → 2026.

```
attributes    date · quantity(int) · costPaid(float) · returnDate
              currency · exchangeRate · currencyAgency · exchangeRateAgency
              canReadAdvantage · canWriteAdvantage
              advantageType {reference:int, name:str}
relationships contract · agency
```

Types observed: « Prime sur lettre d'Objectifs », « Prime exceptionnelle »,
« Prime de vacances », « Titre Restaurant / Indemnité repas », « Abonnement
Transport ».

Three properties that decide a consumer's strategy, all reproduced by the mock:

| Property | Measured | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| no `updateDate` | absent from every row | no cursor exists; a full refresh every run |
| no bulk collection | `/advantages` 403 (WAF), `/resources-advantages` 404, `/contracts-advantages` 404 | one call per resource, structurally |
| sparse | 6 of 8 resources had none | empty is the NORMAL answer, not a degraded one |

## 2. `advantageTypes` — a shape that had never been observed

The mock served it as an always-empty array, so nobody had seen what it carries.
In production: **16 of 18 contracts, 55 lines.**

```
{reference:int, name:str, frequency:str, category:str,
 participationQuota, employeeQuota, agencyQuota}   (float/int)

frequency ∈ {daily, monthly, semiAnnual, annual}
category  ∈ {variableSalaryBasis, package, fixedAmount}
```

It is **not** the `DetailFraisContrat` shape one might have assumed. Three traps,
each measured, each now reproduced by the dataset:

**`reference` is not a global code.** The same `21` is « Health Insurance » on
one contract and « Variable » on another; `5` is « Life Insurance » here and
« Lunch allowance » there. It is agency configuration. A reference table joined
on it would merge unrelated rows.

**`category` does not say what is vested.** Aggregate counts made it look like it
did — `variableSalaryBasis` appeared 5 times, « Prime de vacances » 5 times. The
line-by-line pairing says otherwise:

| name | frequency | category |
|---|---|---|
| « Variable » | annual | **`fixedAmount`** |
| « Prime de vacances » | semiAnnual | **`variableSalaryBasis`** |
| « Primes de vacances (prorata) » | semiAnnual | `fixedAmount` |
| Titre Restaurant · Health Insurance · Lunch Allowance · Abonnement Transport | daily/monthly | `package` |

`category` describes how the amount is expressed and what it feeds — not whether
it is owed. Vested-versus-conditional stays a business call.

**No quota is « the amount ».** Depending on the line, only the employee quota is
set, or employee + agency, or participation + employee. Adding them would
manufacture a number that looks like money. The amount actually paid lives on
`/resources/{id}/advantages`, dated, in `costPaid`.

## 3. Three fields the mock filled and the vendor leaves empty

Measured on 18 contracts. The key **is** emitted; the value is empty.

| Field | Real (filled / served) | Mock before | Mock now |
|---|---|---|---|
| `expensesDetails` | **0 / 18** | 38 of 38, « Titres restaurant », `daily` | `[]` |
| `dailyExpenses` · `monthlyExpenses` | **0 / 18** | 9.05 · 75.0 | 0 · 0 |
| `calendar` | **0 / 18** | `"Standard"` | `""` |
| `activityRate` | **0 / 18** | 100 | 0 |

`activityRate` is the one that already cost something: a consumer weighted an
annual gross by it and shipped 800 salary rows at zero — no error, because a
null salary reads as « this person earns nothing », not as a bug.

`expensesDetails` was on course to repeat it exactly. It is the `isDeleted`
family: green in CI, empty in production.

## 4. Three fields the registry said were never observed — they are

`UNVERIFIED-FIELDS.md` listed these under « documented in the RAML, never
returned »:

| Field | Measured |
|---|---|
| `probationEndDate` | served on 12 of 18, filled on 9 |
| `renewalProbationEndDate` | served on 12 of 18, filled on 4 |
| `contractAverageDailyProductionCost` | served on 1 of 18 |

Emission is **sparse** — the key is absent item by item, not null — and the mock
now omits them the same way. The registry has been corrected.

## 4bis. A formula the mock encoded and production does not follow

The dataset computed `contractAverageDailyCost = monthlySalary * 12 *
chargeFactor / numberOfWorkingDays` — the loaded annual gross spread over
working days. Plausible, tidy, and **wrong**.

Tested against 25 production contracts carrying all four fields:

| | |
|---|---|
| verify within 1 % | **1** |
| do not | **24** |
| median relative error | **11.7 %** |
| worst | **44.2 %** |

The vendor computes something else — most likely actual employer contributions
and benefits included. This is a different species from the empty-field gaps
above: nothing is missing, and every value looks right. The damage is that a
consumer who spots the relation in the mock stops reading `contractAverageDailyCost`
and starts recomputing it, then ships a number that is 12 % off in production
with a green test suite behind it.

The mock now applies a deterministic, per-contract offset that breaks the
relation, and `test_le_cout_journalier_ne_se_derive_pas_du_brut` keeps it broken.

**Consumer-side rule this implies:** read `contractAverageDailyCost`, do not
derive a loaded cost from `chargeFactor`. Both fields are worth extracting;
neither computes the other.

## 5. Fill rates on the contract profile, for sizing

| Filled | Fields |
|---|---|
| 18/18 | `updateDate` · `creationDate` · `startDate` · `numberOfWorkingDays` · `numberOfHoursPerWeek` · `classification` · `exchangeRate` · `exchangeRateAgency` |
| 16–17/18 | `chargeFactor` · `hourlySalary` · `monthlySalary` · `endReason` · `contractAverageDailyCost` |
| sparse | `typeOf` 4/18 · `probationState` 4/18 · `employeeType` 3/18 · `currency` 2/18 · `workingTimeType` 1/18 |
| **0/18** | `activityRate` · `calendar` · `dailyExpenses` · `monthlyExpenses` · `expensesDetails` · `forceHourlySalary` · `partialWorkTimes` · `isPartialWorkTimeEvenOdd` |

`updateDate` at 18/18 is the one that changes a strategy: the contract profile
**does** carry a cursor, so a consumer can merge instead of replacing.

## 6. The payroll apps answer with the ORDINARY client JWT

`docs/EXTRACTION.md` said `/apps/accounting-payroll/contracts` and
`/apps/extract-payroll/contracts` need an app JWT. They do not — the standard
client JWT is enough. What they need is a `month` parameter (`422`, code `1017`,
`source.parameter: month` without it).

```
GET /apps/extract-payroll/contracts?month=2026-08  → 200, totals.rows = 148
attributes: startDate · endDate · typeOf · payrollTerm · payrollComments
            monthlySalary · currency · exchangeRate (+Agency variants)
            productionTimes · internalTimes · absencesTimes
            expensesToPay · expensesAlreadyAdvanced · canGenerateAdvantages
relationships: advantagesToPay · agency · dependsOn · expensesReports · timesReports
```

**`advantagesToPay` is empty on all 148.** It is a « to pay » queue, not a
history — so this route does **not** replace the per-resource calls. Noted here
as a lead for payroll aggregates, not implemented: the mock does not serve these
routes.

## What the mock does about all of this

Dataset `realiste` now carries: variable `advantageTypes` cardinality (0 to 4 per
contract, matching the 0–5 observed), the `reference` collision, the
counter-intuitive name↔category pairing, an `advantages` collection that is
sparse with one long-history witness, sparse probation dates, and empty values
where the vendor leaves them empty.

Locked by `tests/test_ecarts_mesures_en_production.py`.

**Determinism note.** The new generators draw from a *derived* RNG stream, never
the shared one. Adding a draw mid-stream shifted everything generated after it —
measured: time rows went from 61 to 60, with no change to their generator, and a
pagination test failed two files away. Consumers compare identifier SETS; the
world must not move under them.
